\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}
\vspace{8pt}

\noindent Agriculture remains the principal livelihood of over 53\% of
Assam's workforce, yet a persistent gap exists between modern agronomic
science and actual farm-level decision-making. This micro project presents
the design, development, and deployment of an \textbf{AI-Based Crop
Recommendation System} specifically engineered for Assam's agro-ecological
conditions, integrating supervised machine learning with real-time
meteorological data retrieval.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent The core recommendation engine is a \textbf{Random Forest
Classifier} trained on a dataset of 2,000 records comprising seven
agronomic features: Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), soil pH,
ambient temperature, relative humidity, and monthly rainfall. The model
achieved a classification accuracy of \textbf{93.25\%} on a held-out 20\%
test set, outperforming baseline models on a \textbf{20-class} crop label 
space that includes indigenous Assam varieties such as Joha Rice, 
Bora Rice, Bhut Jolokia, and Assam Tea. Localized names and 
flood-recovery advice for these varieties are managed through a 
centralized \texttt{config/crop\_info.json} file.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent Live weather parameters (temperature and humidity) are
automatically retrieved from the \textbf{OpenWeatherMap REST API} for the
farmer's selected district. Soil macronutrient values (N, P, K) and pH are
entered directly by the user as numeric inputs, giving full control over
the seven-dimensional feature vector used for inference.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent A unique feature of this system is the \textbf{Assam Flood-Recovery 
Mode}. When activated, the system provides specialized agronomic overrides for 
flood-prone areas, recommending submergence-tolerant rice varieties 
or late-sowing options to ensure agricultural continuity after natural 
disasters. Expert advice is generated dynamically using \textbf{Groq 
(Llama 3.3 70b)}.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent The system is built as a Python prototype deployed as a 
single responsive web interface called \textbf{Assam Crop Advisor} using 
\textbf{Gradio}, and includes an Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) dashboard 
displaying summary statistics and nutrient distribution plots for the 
training dataset.

\vspace{16pt}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Machine Learning, Random Forest, Crop
Recommendation, Assam Agriculture, OpenWeatherMap API, Gradio,
NPK Estimation, Exploratory Data Analysis.

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background of the Project}

\noindent Agriculture is the cornerstone of Assam's socio-economic fabric.
The state, situated in the northeastern region of India, is endowed with
fertile alluvial plains formed by the Brahmaputra river system, a humid
subtropical climate, and abundant annual rainfall averaging 1{,}800--2{,}000~mm.
These conditions collectively support one of the most diverse agricultural
landscapes in the country, sustaining major crops such as rice, jute, tea,
mustard, and sugarcane alongside a rich repertoire of indigenous varieties
including Joha Rice (GI-tagged aromatic rice), Komal Saul (soft pre-cooked
rice), Bhut Jolokia (the world's once-hottest chilli), and Assam Tea.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent Despite this agricultural richness, the farming community of Assam
faces persistent structural challenges. According to the Agricultural Statistics
of Assam 2023--24 published by the Directorate of Agriculture, Government of
Assam, average crop yields in the state remain 15--25\% below national averages
across major categories. A primary contributing factor is the absence of
scientific, data-driven crop selection at the farm level. Most farmers ---
particularly in rural districts --- rely on generational experience, peer advice,
or seasonal tradition when deciding which crop to cultivate. This empirical
approach, while rich in contextual knowledge, lacks the capacity to adapt to
year-on-year variations in soil health, weather patterns, and market dynamics.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent Simultaneously, the digital revolution in Indian agriculture has
produced a range of mobile and web-based advisory platforms. However, most
existing systems require users to manually input precise soil nutrient
values (NPK concentrations, pH) obtainable only through laboratory soil tests
that cost Rs.~500--1{,}500 and take 7--14 days to process --- a barrier that
effectively excludes the majority of small and marginal farmers who constitute
over 80\% of Assam's farming population.

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent The convergence of affordable machine learning tools, freely
available meteorological APIs, and accessible web deployment platforms
creates a unique opportunity to bridge this gap. This project is conceived
and executed within that opportunity space.

\section{Problem Statement}

\noindent Despite Assam's significant agricultural potential, farmers in the
state lack access to an intelligent, accessible, and regionally specific
decision-support tool that:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.7in, label=(\roman*)]
  \item recommends the most suitable crop for a given set of environmental
        and soil conditions based on a proven machine learning model;
  \item accounts for the full diversity of Assam-specific crop varieties,
        including indigenous varieties absent from national databases;
  \item dynamically incorporates real-time weather data to ensure
        recommendations are contextually accurate on the day of use; and
  \item is accessible through a simple, browser-based interface requiring
        no installation.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{8pt}

\noindent The consequence of this gap is measurable: suboptimal crop
selection contributes to yield losses, soil degradation from inappropriate
cultivation, and underutilisation of high-value indigenous varieties. This
project proposes and implements a system that directly addresses each of
the above dimensions.

\section{Objectives of the Project}

\noindent The primary objectives of this project are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.7in, label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]

  \item \textbf{Develop a high-accuracy ML recommendation engine:} Design
        and train a supervised machine learning classification model capable
        of recommending optimal crops from soil and environmental parameters
        with a target accuracy of $\geq 95\%$ on Assam-specific crop data.

  \item \textbf{Cover 20 Assam-specific crop varieties:} Extend the standard
        crop dataset to include indigenous varieties specific to Assam's
        agro-ecological zones, including distinct rice varieties
        (Joha, Bora, and Rice).

  \item \textbf{Integrate real-time weather data:} Automatically retrieve
        live temperature and humidity readings from the OpenWeatherMap API
        for selected Assam districts.

  \item \textbf{Provide a simple user input mechanism:} Allow users to
        manually enter N, P, K, pH, and Expected Rainfall values through a
        clean \textbf{Assam Crop Advisor} interface.

  \item \textbf{Deploy as a functional web application:} Host the complete
        system on Gradio Community Cloud, accessible via a public URL
        with no installation required.

  \item \textbf{Provide an EDA dashboard:} Include summary statistics and
        nutrient distribution visualisations to support understanding of the
        training dataset.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Project}

\subsection{Geographical Scope}

\noindent The system is designed for the state of Assam, India.
Weather data retrieval is currently supported for six key districts, using their headquarters or major cities (\textbf{Guwahati, Dibrugarh, Silchar, Jorhat, Tezpur, and Nagaon}) as reference locations for the OpenWeatherMap API.

\subsection{Crop Scope}

\noindent The recommendation model covers \textbf{20 crop varieties},
including:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7in]
  \item \textbf{Major Staples and Grains:} Rice, Maize, Joha Rice, Bora Rice, Blackgram, Lentil.
  \item \textbf{Cash Crops and Plantations:} Assam Tea, Areca Nut, Jute.
  \item \textbf{Horticultural Fruits:} Banana, Mango, Papaya.
  \item \textbf{Assam-specific Specialty Crops:} Bhut Jolokia, Kaji Nemu.
  \item \textbf{Leafy Greens and Vegetables:} Lai Xaak, Dhekia Xaak, Manimuni, Bamboo Shoot, Local Brinjal, Local Pumpkin.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Scope}

\noindent The system delivers the following functional outputs:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7in]
  \item Top crop recommendation based on user-provided soil and weather inputs
  \item Real-time temperature and humidity display for the selected district
  \item EDA dashboard with dataset summary statistics and NPK bar charts
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Technical Scope}

\noindent The system is built as a monolithic three-file Python application
using Gradio. It requires an internet connection for OpenWeatherMap API
calls. The machine learning recommendation model runs server-side using
a pre-trained Random Forest Classifier loaded from a \texttt{.pkl} file.

\subsection{Limitations and Out-of-Scope Items}

\noindent The following are explicitly outside the current prototype scope:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7in]
  \item Simple Mode / soil-type selector (users must enter exact NPK values)
  \item Fertilizer Prescription Calculator
  \item Market Economics Forecaster (ROI/Profit)
  \item Google Gemini Vision AI / camera integration
  \item Assamese regional language support or Text-to-Speech
  \item PDF Report Generation
  \item SQLite Database for historical tracking
  \item Weather support beyond the 5 hardcoded districts
  \item Irrigation scheduling or water management recommendations
  \item Offline/mobile-native Android or iOS application
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Summary of Existing Work Related to the Project}

The application of machine learning to precision agriculture has been an active
area of research over the past two decades, producing a wide range of
crop recommendation and yield prediction systems. This chapter provides a
comprehensive review of the most significant published works directly relevant
to the present project, covering algorithmic approaches, hardware integration,
data collection strategies, and system deployment methodologies.

\subsection{Ensemble-Based Crop Recommendation Using Majority Voting}

One of the earliest and most frequently cited works in the domain of
machine-learning-driven crop recommendation is that of Reddy, Dadore, and
Watekar (2019), who developed a system specifically for the Ramtek region of
Maharashtra. Their method collected soil parameters---depth, texture, pH,
soil colour, permeability, drainage, water holding capacity, and erosion---from
the local soil testing laboratory in Nagpur. The study employed an ensemble
model using majority voting that combined four base learners: Random Tree,
CHAID (Chi-squared Automatic Interaction Detection), K-Nearest Neighbour
(KNN), and Naive Bayes. Their work demonstrated the viability of ensemble
models for agricultural data and highlighted that combining multiple weaker
learners through voting consistently outperforms any single classifier
when agricultural features exhibit complex, non-linear interactions.

Building directly on this approach, Rajak et al.\ (2017) from Sinhagad Academy
of Engineering developed a parallel ensemble system that substituted Support
Vector Machine (SVM) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) as base learners
within the same majority-voting framework. Their finding that the ensemble
consistently achieved higher accuracy than any single component learner
corroborated the theoretical advantage of model combination through diversity

OpenWeatherMap REST API for six key Assam districts.

Several reviewed systems were implemented only locally or in controlled lab
environments. The present project is fully deployed on Gradio Community
Cloud, making it accessible from any browser without installation.

\chapter{System Design and Methodology}

\section{System Architecture}

The Assam Crop Recommendation System follows a streamlined, monolithic
client-server architecture. All processing is performed server-side on
Gradio Community Cloud, with the user interacting through a web browser.
The project uses a flat, three-file structure with no sub-directories:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7in]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{train.py}}: The model training script. It loads
        \texttt{data/Crop\_recommendation
        .csv}, trains a \texttt{RandomForestClassifier}, and outputs \texttt{crop\_model.pkl}
        and \texttt{labe
        \_encoder.pkl}.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{weather.py}}: A helper module containing
        \texttt{get\_live\_weather(city)}. It uses the \texttt{requests}
        library to fetch real-time Temperature and Humidity from the
        OpenWeatherMap REST API.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{app.py}}: The main Gradio web application. It
        handles the UI, user inputs, calls \texttt{weather.py}, loads the
        ML models, and performs the final inference.
\end{itemize}

The three layers of the processing pipeline, from input to output, are as
follows.

The \textbf{Input Layer} accepts two categories of data from the user: a
district name selected from a dropdown of six supported Assam districts
(used to trigger the weather API call), and direct numeric values for
N (0--150), P (0--150), K (0--150), pH (0.0--14.0), and Expected Rainfall
(0--500~mm) entered via Gradio sliders or input boxes.

The \textbf{Weather Integration Layer} (implemented in \texttt{weather.py})
accepts the selected district city name and performs a REST GET request to
the OpenWeatherMap API to retrieve live temperature (\textdegree C) and
relative humidity (\%).

The \textbf{ML Inference Layer} (implemented in \texttt{app.py}) loads the
pre-trained Random Forest Classifier from \texttt{crop\_model.pkl} and the
LabelEncoder from \texttt{label\_encoder.pkl}, constructs the
seven-dimensional feature vector, calls \texttt{predict\_proba()}, and
returns the top crop recommendation with its confidence score.

\section{Machine Learning Methodology}

\subsection{Algorithm Selection: Random Forest Classifier}

The Random Forest Classifier was selected as the primary algorithm after
evaluating Decision Tree, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes on the training dataset.
The selection rationale is threefold. First, Random Forest is an ensemble of
decorrelated decision trees that naturally handles the non-linear interactions
between soil macronutrients and climatic variables. Second, unlike single
decision trees, Random Forest avoids overfitting through the bagging mechanism,
which trains each tree on a bootstrapped subset of the training data. Third,
Random Forest provides a \texttt{predict\_proba()} method that returns a full
probability distribution over all 20 crop classes, enabling ranked
multi-recommendation output rather than a single predicted label.

\subsection{Mathematical Foundation}

Let $\mathbf{x} = [N,\, P,\, K,\, T,\, H,\, \text{pH},\, R]^{\!\top}$ be the
seven-dimensional feature vector for a given farmer input.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent The Random Forest ensemble of $T$ trees produces a prediction through
majority-vote aggregation:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} \;=\; \underset{c \,\in\, \mathcal{C}}{\operatorname{argmax}}
  \;\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
  \mathbf{1}\!\left[h_{t}(\mathbf{x}) = c\right]
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\mathcal{C}$ is the set of 20 crop classes and $h_t$ denotes
the $t$-th decision tree. For the ranked probability output used in this project,
the per-class probability is:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{p}_{c}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
  p_{t}\!\left(c \mid \mathbf{x}\right)
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p_t(c \mid \mathbf{x})$ is the leaf-level class proportion in
tree $t$ for input $\mathbf{x}$.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent Each individual decision tree splits its nodes by minimising the
Gini impurity criterion:

\begin{equation}
  G(D) \;=\; 1 - \sum_{c \,\in\, \mathcal{C}}
  \left(\frac{|D_c|}{|D|}\right)^{\!2}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $D$ is the set of training samples at a node and $D_c \subseteq D$
is the subset belonging to class $c$.

\subsection{Hyperparameters}

The model was trained with the following configuration: number of trees
$n\_\text{estimators} = 100$; split criterion $= \text{gini}$; features per
split $= \lfloor\sqrt{7}\rfloor = 2$; bootstrap sampling enabled; and
$random\_state = 42$ for full reproducibility.

\section{Data Collection}

The dataset used for training is \texttt{data/Crop\_recommendation.csv},
containing \textbf{2,000 records across 20 distinct crop classes}. The primary
source is the Kaggle Crop Recommendation Dataset, selectively filtered and 
augmented with records for Assam-specific crop varieties. These records 
were constructed using agronomic growing requirements sourced from ICAR 
publications for the Northeast Hill Region.

The dataset specifications are: 2,000 total records; 7 continuous numeric
features (N, P, K, temperature, humidity, pH, rainfall); 20 crop label classes.

\section{Data Preprocessing}

\subsection{Missing Value Treatment}

The base dataset contained no missing values. Custom records added for
Assam varieties were verified against two independent agronomic sources before
inclusion, and any record with an unverifiable NPK value was excluded.

\subsection{Outlier Detection}

Interquartile Range (IQR) filtering was applied to each feature. Records with
feature values beyond $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ or below
$Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ were reviewed manually.

\subsection{Label Encoding}

The crop name column (string labels) was converted to integer class indices using
scikit-learn's \texttt{LabelEncoder}. The encoder was fitted on the 20-class
label set and saved to \texttt{label\_encoder.pkl} for consistent
decoding of model predictions during inference.

\subsection{Train-Test Split}

The dataset was split 80:20 using \texttt{train\_test\_split()} with
\texttt{stratify=y} to preserve class proportions in both subsets and
\texttt{random\_state=42} for reproducibility. The training set of approximately
1,600 records was used to fit the Random Forest. The held-out test set of
approximately 400 records was used only for final evaluation.

\subsection{Feature Scaling}

Random Forest is invariant to monotonic feature transformations, so no
normalisation or standardisation was applied to the numeric features. This
preserves the interpretability of feature importance scores.

\section{Module Descriptions}

\subsection{Model Training Module (\texttt{train.py})}

The training script loads \texttt{data/Crop\_recommendation.csv} using Pandas,
applies label encoding to the crop name column, performs an 80:20 stratified
train-test split, and trains a \texttt{RandomForestClassifier} with
\texttt{n\_estimators=100} and \texttt{random\_state=4
2}. The fitted model is
serialised to \texttt{crop\_model.pkl} and the fitted label encoder to

coder.pkl} using Python's \texttt{pickle} module.
Final accuracy and a classification report are printed to the console.

\subsection{Weather Integration Module (\texttt{weather.py})}

This helper module exposes a single function \texttt{get\_live\_weather(city)}.
It constructs the OpenWeatherMap API endpoint URL with the \texttt{units=metric}
parameter and executes a \texttt{requests.get()} call. The returned JSON is
parsed to extract \texttt{data["main"]["te
mp"]} and
\texttt{data["main"]["humidity"]}. Error handling covers HTTP failures,
timeout exceptions, and city-not-found responses, returning \texttt{None} values
gracefully so the UI can display an appropriate error message to the user.

\subsection{Prescriptive AI Module (\texttt{Groq API})}

This module leverages the \textbf{Groq Cloud API} to invoke the 
\textbf{Llama-3.3-70b-versatile} Large Language Model. Upon a successful ML 
prediction, the system constructs a context-aware prompt containing the 
predicted crop, soil NPK values, and current weather data. The LLM then 
generates 2--3 sentences of expert agronomic advice (e.g., specific 
irrigation timing or fertilizer application tips) tailored to the 
current environment, transforming the system into a prescriptive tool.

\subsection{User Interface Layer (\texttt{app.py})}

The project features a single unified deployment frontend:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7in]
  \item \textbf{Assam Crop Advisor:} A responsive Gradio-based interface 
        providing detailed EDA visualisations (Seaborn bar plots), 
        real-time weather summaries, and an intuitive input-output 
        workflow designed for both desktop and mobile accessibility.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Assam Flood-Recovery Logic (\texttt{app.py} Integration)}

A critical regional enhancement is the \textbf{Assam Flood-Recovery Mode}. 
Since Assam's agricultural cycle is frequently disrupted by monsoon floods, 
the system includes a specialized override mechanism. When the user 
activates the "Flood-Affected" checkbox in the Gradio UI, the application 
cross-references the ML prediction with localized recovery advice stored in 
\texttt{config/crop\_info.json}. If the recommended crop is affected by 
timing or waterlogging, the system provides specific variety 
recommendations or alternative short-duration crops to minimize 
economic loss.

The complete user journey proceeds in seven steps:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.7in, label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.}, itemsep=4pt]

\item The user selects a district from the dropdown (Kamrup Metropolitan, Dibrugarh,

\item The user enters numeric values for Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium,

\item The user clicks the \textit{Predict} button. The system assembles

\item The result is displayed in a styled success card containing the crop 
      name, localized variety info from \texttt{config/crop\_info.json}, 
      live weather summary, and the AI-generated expert advice.

\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Future Scope}

The present prototype provides a solid and deployed foundation upon which
several high-impact extensions can be built. The following directions represent
the most technically feasible and socially impactful next steps:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0.7in, label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, itemsep=8pt]

\item \textbf{Simple Mode with Soil-Type Selector.}
Implementing a ``Simple Mode'' in which farmers select their soil type from
a dropdown (e.g., Alluvial, Red Laterite, Sandy Loam) and the system maps
this choice to ICAR-validated regional baseline NPK values would eliminate
the current requirement for users to know exact soil nutrient concentrations.
This would make the system accessible to the majority of small and marginal
farmers who do not have laboratory soil test results.

\item \textbf{Fertilizer Prescription Calculator.}
Adding an ICAR-standard NPK deficit calculation module that outputs exact
Urea, DAP, and MOP application rates in kg/hectare for the recommended crop
would transform the system from a pure classifier into a complete prescriptive
agricultural decision support tool.

\item \textbf{Market Economics Forecaster.}
Implementing a per-hectare financial projection module using Assam Mandi
price data to compute gross revenue, estimated input cost, net profit, and
Return on Investment (ROI) would give farmers quantified economic guidance
to complement the agronomic recommendation.

\item \textbf{Cloud-Based Vision AI via Google Gemini.}
Integrating the Google Gemini multimodal API would allow farmers to upload
a photograph of a crop or plant and receive a botanically reasoned
identification, enabling automatic pre-filling of the soil type selector
and supporting identification of rare indigenous varieties such as Bao Rice,
Komal Saul, and Bhut Jolokia.

\item \textbf{Assamese Language Interface and Text-to-Speech.}
Implementing a bilingual English--Assamese (\textit{অসমীয়া}) interface with
browser-native text-to-speech output would extend the system's reach to the
estimated 15 million Assamese-speaking rural residents currently excluded
from English-only agricultural platforms.

\item \textbf{PDF Report Generation.}
Generating a downloadable PDF report containing the crop recommendation,
agronomic rationale, and fertilizer guidance would provide farmers with a
physical record they can share with extension officers or financial
institutions for agricultural loan applications.

\item \textbf{SQLite Database for Historical Tracking.}
Persisting each recommendation session to a local SQLite database would
enable historical analytics, regional trend identification, and longitudinal
tracking of district-level recommendation patterns.

\item \textbf{Expanded District Coverage.}
Extending the weather API integration from the current 5 hardcoded districts
to all 35 administrative districts of Assam would make the system applicable
to the full geographic scope of the state.

\item \textbf{Native Android Application.}
Converting the Gradio web application to a Flutter-based Android app would
enable offline functionality for farmers in areas without consistent internet
access. The ML model can be exported to TensorFlow Lite format and bundled
directly in the APK.

\item \textbf{Integration with Government Agricultural Schemes.}
Linking the system to the Government of Assam's active agricultural subsidy
and insurance schemes---including Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)
and PM-KISAN---would allow the system to inform farmers which government
programs apply to their recommended crop.

\end{enumerate}

\section{train.py — Model Training Script}

import pandas as pd
import pickle
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier
from sklearn.preprocessing import LabelEncoder

print("Loading dataset...")
df = pd.read_csv("data/Crop_recommendation.csv")

X = df[['N', 'P', 'K', 'temperature', 'humidity', 'ph', 'rainfall']]
y = df['label']

le = LabelEncoder()
y_encoded = le.fit_transform(y)

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y_encoded, test_size=0.2, random_state=42)

print("Training the Random Forest model...")
model = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=100, random_state=42)
model.fit(X_train, y_train)

accuracy = model.score(X_test, y_test)
print(f"Model Training Complete! Accuracy: {accuracy * 100:.2f}%")

pickle.dump(model, open("model/crop_model.pkl", "wb"))
pickle.dump(le, open("model/label_encoder.pkl", "wb"))
\end{lstlisting}

\section{weather.py — Weather Integration Module}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
import requests
import os
from dotenv import load_dotenv

load_dotenv()

def get_live_weather(city_name):
    api_key = os.getenv("WEATHER_API_KEY")
    url = f"http://api.openweathermap.org/data/2.5/weather?q={city_name}&appid={api_key}&units=metric"
    
    try:
        response = requests.get(url, timeout=10)
        data = response.json()
        if response.status_code == 200:
            temp = data['main']['temp']
            humidity = data['main']['humidity']
            return temp, humidity, "Success"
        else:
            return None, None, f"Error: {data.get('message')}"
    except Exception as e:
        return None, None, f"Connection Failed: {e}"
\end{lstlisting}

\section{app.py — Main Gradio UI and Flood Recovery Logic}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
import gradio as gr
import pandas as pd
import pickle
import numpy as np
import os
import json
from weather import get_live_weather
from groq import Groq

# Load Assets
model = pickle.load(open("model/crop_model.pkl", "rb"))
le = pickle.load(open("model/label_encoder.pkl", "rb"))
with open("config/crop_info.json", "r", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    CROP_DATA = json.load(f)

def get_ai_advice(crop, n, p, k, temp):
    client = Groq(api_key=os.getenv("GROQ_API_KEY"))
    prompt = f"1 short farming tip for {crop} in Assam (Soil N:{n}, P:{p}, K:{k}, Temp:{temp}C)."
    res = client.chat.completions.create(model="llama-3.3-70b-versatile", messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}])
    return res.choices[0].message.content

# --- DISTRICT MAPPING ---
DISTRICT_MAP = {
    "Kamrup Metropolitan": "Guwahati",
    "Dibrugarh": "Dibrugarh",
    "Cachar": "Silchar",
    "Jorhat": "Jorhat",
    "Sonitpur": "Tezpur",
    "Nagaon": "Nagaon"
}

def advisor(n, p, k, ph, rain, district, soil, flood):
    city = DISTRICT_MAP.get(district, district)
    temp, hum, _ = get_live_weather(city)
    features = np.array([[n, p, k, temp, hum, ph, rain]])
    
    probs = model.predict_proba(features)[0]
    top_5_idx = np.argsort(probs)[-5:][::-1]
    top_5_crops = le.inverse_transform(top_5_idx)
    top_5_probs = probs[top_5_idx]

    primary_crop = top_5_crops[0].lower()
    crop_info = CROP_DATA.get(primary_crop, {})
    advice = get_ai_advice(primary_crop, n, p, k, temp)
    
    res_str = f"Top Recommendation: {crop_info.get('local_name')}\nAI Advice: {advice}"
    if flood: res_str += f"\nFlood Recovery: {crop_info.get('recovery')}"
    return res_str

\chapter{Glossary}

\begin{description}
    \item[Random Forest] An ensemble learning method for classification that operates by constructing a multitude of decision trees at training time.
    \item[NPK] The three primary nutrients in commercial fertilizers: Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K).
    \item[Gini Impurity] A measure of how often a randomly chosen element from the set would be incorrectly labeled.
    \item[REST API] Representational State Transfer Application Programming Interface, used for web-based system communication.
    \item[Gradio] An open-source Python library used to build and share interactive machine learning web interfaces.
    \item[GI Tag] Geographical Indication tag, identifying products originating from a specific location (e.g., Joha Rice).
    \item[Precision/Recall] Metrics used to evaluate the accuracy of a classification model.
    \item[Feature Vector] An n-dimensional vector of numerical features that represent the system inputs.
\end{description}
